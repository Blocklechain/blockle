    BLOCK vs wBLOCK

    BLOCK is the native coin of the Blockle post-quantum L1 (8 decimals, block1… addresses). It exists only on the Blockle chain.

    wBLOCK is a standard SPL token on Solana (also 8 decimals) that represents BLOCK. It is minted when BLOCK is deposited to the bridge and burned when wBLOCK is redeemed back to BLOCK.

    How a bridge works here

    BLOCK→wBLOCK: you send BLOCK to the bridge address; after enough confirmations the operator mints the equivalent wBLOCK to your Solana address.

    wBLOCK→BLOCK: you burn wBLOCK to the bridge; the operator then releases BLOCK from the reserve to your Blockle address.

    ⚠ Risks — read before bridging

    • Custodial trust. wBLOCK is only as safe as the operator. If the operator's reserve or keys are lost, compromised, or mismanaged, wBLOCK can lose its backing. There is no on-chain vault or smart-contract guarantee enforcing 1:1 backing.
    • Wrapped-token risk. wBLOCK is not BLOCK. Its value depends entirely on the bridge honoring redemptions. A wrapped asset can trade below its backing or become non-redeemable.
    • Cross-chain risk. Bridges are a common target for exploits. Transfers cross two independent networks and are irreversible once confirmed. Always double-check the destination address and the direction.
    • No finality from a signature. Signing a transaction in your wallet only submits it. A bridge transfer is complete only after the source chain confirms and the mint/release lands on the destination chain. Watch the status stages.
    • Operator discretion & limits. Per-transfer and daily limits apply. The operator may pause the bridge. Funds sent outside the supported flow may be unrecoverable.
    Reserve balances, wrapped supply, fees and limits are read live from the bridge service — figures shown without live data are not estimates and are omitted. The operator never asks for your private keys or seed phrase. Bridge status and history are public on the transactions explorer and reserves page.
